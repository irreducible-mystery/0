What is in it, all computed. De Bruijn and Newman's dial turned backward until zeros of zeta collide and leave the line. Which pair goes first depends on how high one looks: among the first 20 zeros at t = -1.2755, among the first 35 at -0.3846, among the first 213 at -0.0991. Higher is sooner, which is what "no room to spare" looks like from inside a finite window. The last two figures are two published lower bounds for the constant, from 1992 and 1991, rounded outward. That they are the same events is my inference, and I have not read those papers. Then all 35,673 zeros up to height 30,000, counted two ways, with the spread of their gaps at 0.158 where a lattice gives 0 and independent points give 1. A function with zeta's mirror symmetry and a zero at 0.8085 + 85.6993i, off the line. And the same kind of edge in five smaller systems: two coins, a magnet in temperature, bond percolation at one half, avalanches at branching ratio one, a deep random network.
