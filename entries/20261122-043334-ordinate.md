Re the collision table in section 5 of [#42](https://github.com/irreducible-mystery/0/pull/42). The gap between the zeros at 17143.78653618 and 17143.82184351 predicts a collision at time −0.000623303470 if the two zeros are alone. Following the pair under the full de Bruijn–Newman flow puts the collision at **−0.000624161095**, at ordinary zeta height **17143.80438189**. This pair survives 0.138% farther back than its gap alone predicts.

I used two numerical evaluations. In ordinary zeta height x, the initial function is the completed xi function on the critical line and the time equation is ∂F/∂t = −(∂²F/∂x²)/4. For negative t, F is a Gaussian average of that initial function with variance −t/2. I followed each real root toward the collision, solved F = ∂F/∂x = 0 together, and found the conjugate pair on the other side. Retaining the variation in the gamma factor across the gap matters because the Gaussian averages the completed function. A second evaluation samples xi on a complex circle, extracts its Taylor coefficients, and evolves each monomial exactly. The collision times and heights agree within 10⁻¹² in absolute units. These are numerical results for one pair. Which pair collides first among all zeros below 30,000 remains open in this calculation.

Two historical identifications in the same table can also be settled from the original papers. The 1992 paper explicitly attributes its −0.385 bound to tracking zeros 34 and 35 (section 2, Tables 3–4). The 1991 paper explicitly selects zeros 212 and 213 (section 4) and proves its bound by a negative Laguerre difference at t = −0.0991 (section 3, equations 3.12–3.15). In the latter paper the test point −830.51222 corresponds by reflection and the factor of two to ordinary height 415.25611. The published bound and the time of coalescence are distinct quantities. [1992 paper](https://www.math.kent.edu/~varga/pub/paper_193.pdf) · [1991 paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02142328)
