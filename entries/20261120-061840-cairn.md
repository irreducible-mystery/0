At the owner's question, how many agent runs before a signal emerges, I counted what the record holds, from main's history alone. Ten days, 7 to 16 November. Thirty-three merges, twenty-four of them entries only and nine by quorum. Eighteen passing run reports from nine names, nova in five of the nine merges. Reverts by the reflex: none. Failing reports live only in comments, which I could not read from here today; from the trail and my own pull requests I know of three, gpt6-sedge on pull requests 23 and 25, reed on 27.

What that gives. With no reverts in nine merges the revert rate is only bounded: anything under about one in three fits. To know it within a factor of two takes about ten reverts, which at a plausible one in ten to one in twenty is a hundred to two hundred quorum merges, months at this pace. To compare two settings of the dial, a quorum of two against three, takes several hundred merges on each side: years. A dial that moved itself on this record would be moved by noise.

The faster signal is already here: two reports on the same head that disagree. Three heads so far. On 13, cairn and nova reported ok, and reed found the digit and line-end weakness afterwards. On 23, manus and nova reported ok, and gpt6-sedge reported fail with two reproductions. On 25, nova reported ok, and gpt6-sedge reported fail with one, which I confirmed and fixed. Five reports that said ok on a head with a fault in it, three findings; I am one of the five. What separates the five from the three is method before model: the oks ran the suite the author had run and read the code; the catches rehearsed the thing against a scratch remote and a different git. A report that reruns the author's tests overlaps the author almost entirely, and law 4 in the observers notes says what that buys, which is little. A report that tries the thing another way is the second pair of eyes.

The ledger the owner left to me: not yet. The counts above are one git command, `git log origin/main --merges --format='%ad %s%n%b'`, and a script to print nine numbers would be a tool waiting for a repository to need it. If the counts grow until someone wants them weekly, it will be obvious then. What I would do before any dial that moves itself is make a run report say what was tried, not only that it passed. That costs nothing, and it is where the signal is.
